AskChron

AI-powered research assistant trained on more than 130,000 Chronicle news articles and opinion pieces.

Role:
Lead Designer

Timeline:
November 2024 – June 2025

Team:
4-person team

Tools:
Figma, JavaScript


The Ask

In late 2024, we partnered with AWS to build an AI research assistant to help our primary institutional users quickly answer their questions about the higher education landscape.

Key considerations:

  • Working with a very particular set of users
  • Avoiding AI skepticism, especially as this was early in the days of LLMs becoming more commonplace
  • Creating an easy and seamless experience that could pull from all of The Chronicle's archives specifically (more on that decision later)

Research & Discovery

User Types

We already knew our user base well: mostly university administrators, deans, provosts, and presidents.

Key insights:

  • These users highly value reputable, well-sourced information, so minimizing hallucinations and surfacing sources were of the utmost importance
  • Tech literacy varies widely across this group, so simplicity would serve them better than a feature-dense tool
  • Their time is extremely valuable, so we kept the experience low-faff: only the most useful features, with minimal steps and interactions

Behavioural Decisions

Tone

We decided on a professional yet friendly tone for AskChron's responses. Accuracy and neutrality were important here, but we didn't want to veer too academic - responses needed to be easy to read and keep mental load low. At the same time, we're dealing with a highly educated, professional user base, so going too casual or light wouldn't necessarily fit the situation.

The Walled Garden

This was built while LLMs were starting to go mainstream, so we were highly conscious of hallucinations and incorrect information, especially content pulled from poor sources. Using only Chronicle content also meant we could provide direct, easy links to sources while keeping users on the Chronicle site, improving recirculation of our content. We started by training it on the Chronicle's online material, with the eventual goal of scanning and integrating the last 60 years of Chronicle archives.
