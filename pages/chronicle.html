Design System & Rebranding

A year-long initiative with the engineering team to migrate our CMS theme to eliminate design and tech debt, add new building blocks and functionality for our editorial teams, and provide an overall visual refresh to Chronicle.com. This ended with a whole visual overhaul to Chronicle.com.

This project started with an audit of the design system, CMS components and codebase to see what we could simplify, clean up, and see what to bring over and what to remove or change.

The development team and I started work with updating values for typography, color, and other design tokens in the global styles before moving on to styling and working through hundreds of CMS modules, one piece at a time.

The before and after of Chronicle.com's home page, showcasing several of the visual and systemic updates.

Partway through this project, we received new branding guidelines. We had to rethink how we structured and applied the LESS and variables; however, due to the earlier auditing at the beginning of the project, this was a much quicker project that validated our success in eliminating tech and design debt.

Throughout this process, I also created a thorough digital design QA document to be used across the company (see one page of this document below).

Header & Footer System Design

A header redesign project I lead that resulted in a new header system focused on serving users’ needs as they navigate different types of pages, a redesigned footer, and the groundwork for a navigation system rework.

I started by gaining insights on how users navigate the site and header through Adobe Analytics before beginning ideation. We settled on a design that went partially through development, but then went through multiple iterations as we received new branding guidelines which gave us justification for starting the project from square 1.

Starting from scratch gave me the chance to conduct design workshops with key business stakeholders and C-suite executives. These workshops were focused exclusively on analysis, goals, and priorities; stakeholders were encouraged to avoid any solution ideation. We were able to come out of the series with firm requirements, scope, and a goal priority list.

×
The Figjam board after the first design workshop.

Figjam showing the process of the stakeholder design workshops.

For the header’s functionality, I settled on a responsive and customizable system, with emphasis on serving the user’s goals on each page. This header system consisted of two core parts, the “core header” and the “slim header”. The “core header” would be present at the top of pages, but on scroll would be replaced by the sticky “slim header” that is customizable for each page type and provide different featured options for the user while maintaining but minimizing the core options that users would need on each page.

Accessibility Audits & Compliance

I conducted a thorough accessibility audit, based on WCAG accessibility standards. I checked for contrast, keyboard and screen reader accessibility, technical accessibility, and more. I presented my findings to department heads and was responsible for providing suggestions to remedy the discovered issues to bring us up to minimum AA compliance (AAA whenever possible).

Throughout my tenure, I consistently advised and implemented best accessibility practices on all products across print and digital.

Ask Chron AI

A large language model research assistant that can answer higher education related questions with information from sources published on chronicle.com.

I was tasked with designing the user interface and helping with determining key user features and requirements; such as the ability to save messages, provide feedback in multiple ways, and view sources directly on Chronicle.com. We went through multiple iterations of UI before releasing the beta version in September 2024, followed by an open version in January 2025.

Higher Ed Pay Tracker

The Higher Ed Pay Tracker is a data visualization tool that I worked with the Chronicle Intelligence team to refine the user experience and interface of the tool. I conducted user interviews with subscribers such as university presidents, deans, and other administrators to better understand how they might use the tool, what they may use the tool for, and asked them to perform certain tasks to test the usability of the current iteration.
