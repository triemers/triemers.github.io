TL;DR

Lost Canyon Imports is an ecommerce brand that sells traditional, endangered, artisan-made crafts from South America, with the goal of bringing artisans' work to the US market, increasing demand and allowing the artisans to hire and train apprentices to continue their craft. I helped create this brand from scratch as part of a team of 3, and was the only technology contributor. I was heavily involved in branding as well as completely responsible for site design and implementation.

🔬 Research phase included: competitive analysis, extensive market research, and user interviews to nail down our target audiences.

🎨 Design and ideation phases involved: moodboards, branding and styleguide development, user flow sketches, information architecture trees, and paper sketches.

💻 Implementation looked like: CMS implementation; I created the structure and styles for the site, including a CSS stylesheet. I also contributed some to the copy and ensured accessibility and SEO.

🏆 Results show: we created an effective and sustainable ecommerce storefront to complement the market and gallery side of the company; resulting in more sales, higher conversion rate, and significantly wider market reach. The company is having a notable impact for the partner artisans, most of which have since been able to expand their workshops as a result of the demand.

Market & Competitive Research

This business was being built from scratch, which gave me the unique opportunity of being around for all of the initial market research.

Competitive research involved looking at brands large and small in the same space. I looked for visual design trends, how these companies organized products, and tried to get an idea of who they were marketing to and how.

Looking at their specialties also allowed us to define our niche and value prop; high quality, authentic, small batch endangered art. Communicating this to our users and customers became a priority.

I worked with the owner to conduct some initial user surveys to both gauge interest in the product, but also get a general idea of the types of people that would likely be our primary users.

We gained a good amount of information from initial surveys, and opted to interview several respondents to further understand their interest in the product as well as their online shopping habits.

Scope & Requirements

As I would not be permanently maintaining this, and the owner wished to scale before hiring someone permanent for handling the technology aspect of the business, I knew I would have to use a CMS for the sales platform.

I chose Squarespace for the price and integrations, as well as ease of maintenance as the owner had minimal technology experience at the start.

For setup, I chose to make a simple CSS stylesheet, but overall use the WYSIWYG block editor so that items such as images and text would be easy to update by the owner with minimal risk of breaking.

Information Architecture & Purchase Flows

Due to the nature of selling unique items, Lost Canyon has the problem of having a lot of different SKUs. There are lots of unique variants for each item, which would quickly become overwhelming if listed all at once.

We looked to competitors to see how others organized their products. We then performed a categorization exercise with a small group of potential users and customers to see where they might expect to find certain products, and based the organization of products off of the categories that were created.

The purchase flow was fairly simple and did not have much room for customization in the CMS, but we were still able to add some touches to smooth out the purchasing experience, such as defining the cart as a button and adding quick buy options.

The quick buy option was implemented after release, after I had noticed that we had users dropping off in the middle of the purchase flow. It took some additional CSS to make work correctly, but ultimately lead to an increase in conversions when implemented.

Additional Artifacts & Live UX

In order to help promote the website, as well as the documentaries about the artisans that the owner had made, I designed simple placards and handouts that gave information about the crafts, artisans, and linked to the Youtube videos.

I also designed basic packaging and care/information sheets for each product line that would be included with all purchases. These were to ensure that customers would have the historical and cultural context and information that was essential to the brand, as well as answer questions they may have about caring for each piece.

Lost Canyon does much of their business at museums, markets and other live events, so it was important to consider the UX and marketing in person.

We worked on sample "user flows" through and tested over the course of several markets and one long-standing gallery, using observation of users and conversion/sales metrics to help make decisions.

Launch & Impact

Lost Canyon launched in 2021, and sales continue to grow rapidly each year. Online sales now account for about half of sales and have been crucial to scaling up the business to a sustainable level.

Optimizing the in-person user experience has lead to 30-60% higher sales (depending on which events and markets, measured across yearly events).

The biggest impact thus far; the increase in demand, particularly in tamo, mask-making and silver filigree, has led to the artisans we work with to expand their shops and hire more apprentices. The company is now looking at adding additional artisans and crafts to their roster and expanding to other countries in South America.
