The Mission

Lost Canyon Imports was founded with a social mission: preserve endangered traditional crafts from South America by connecting artisans directly with U.S. customers. When demand increases, artisans can hire apprentices and pass down techniques that might otherwise disappear within a generation.

I joined as the only technology contributor on a 3-person founding team, responsible for turning this vision into a functional e-commerce experience. This wasn't just about selling products; it was about telling stories that would inspire customers to become advocates for cultural preservation.

The Challenge

Starting from absolute zero
Unlike most design projects, we had no existing product, brand, or customer base. Every decision from color palette to checkout flow needed to be made from scratch.

Key constraints I had to navigate:

  • Limited technical resources: The owner had minimal tech experience and no budget for ongoing development support
  • Complex inventory: Each handcrafted item was unique with multiple variants (size, color, pattern), creating hundreds of SKUs that could easily overwhelm customers
  • Dual sales channels: The business operated both online and at in-person markets/galleries, requiring a holistic UX approach across physical and digital spaces
  • Cultural authenticity: The brand identity needed to honor the artisans' cultures without appropriation or cliché
  • Sustainability requirement: Whatever I built needed to be maintainable by a non-technical owner after my departure

Discovery: Understanding the Market & Users

Competitive Analysis

I analyzed both large and small competitors in the artisan craft e-commerce space, looking at:

  • Visual design trends and brand positioning
  • Product organization strategies
  • Target audience messaging and tone
  • How competitors communicated value propositions
Key insight: Most competitors focused on aesthetics or price. Our unique value prop was to lead with authenticity, craft heritage, and social impact. We needed to make customers feel like cultural preservationists, not just shoppers.

User Research

Working with the owner, I designed and conducted: Initial surveys to gauge product interest and understand our potential customers' demographics, values, and shopping behaviors Follow-up interviews with survey respondents who showed strong interest, diving deeper into:

  • Their motivations for purchasing artisan goods
  • Online shopping habits and pain points
  • What made them trust a brand they'd never heard of
  • How much cultural context they wanted when shopping
Key insights:
  • Our target audience valued authenticity and wanted to know the artisan's stories
  • They were willing to pay premium prices if they understood the craftsmanship and impact
  • They were concerned about "fake" artisan goods and exploitative practices
  • They wanted confidence that their purchase was truly helping preserve traditions

Brand Development: Authenticity Meets Adventure

Visual Identity Strategy

The brand needed to feel adventurous (honoring the "lost" cultural treasures we were bringing to light) while maintaining respect and authenticity. Working with the team, I created a moodboard that captured this balance.

Inspiration direction:1920s-30s expedition aesthetic meets Latin American cultural richness—think vintage explorer journals mixed with vibrant craft traditions, not Indiana Jones colonialism.

Color Palette from Craft

Rather than choosing colors arbitrarily, I pulled the core palette directly from a handwoven runner sample we'd received from one of our artisan partners. This approach:

  • Grounded the brand in the actual crafts we were selling
  • Ensured authenticity in color choices
  • Created immediate visual connection between brand and products

Brand Principles

We established three core brand values that informed every design decision:

  • Authenticity:No gimmicks, no fake stories, transparent and ethical sourcing
  • Tradition:Respect for craft heritage and cultural significance
  • Adventure:Discovery, exploration, and the thrill of finding something rare

These principles shaped our voice, typography choices, social media presence, and visual design language.

Technical Strategy: Balancing Functionality & Maintainability

Platform Selection

I evaluated several e-commerce platforms with specific criteria:

  • Non-technical owner must be able to make updates independently
  • Budget-conscious pricing for a startup
  • Enough design flexibility to create a custom brand experience

Decision: Squarespace While more limited than custom development, Squarespace offered the right balance of ease-of-use, integrations, and visual flexibility for our needs.

Development Approach

I created a custom CSS stylesheet to control the visual design while using Squarespace's WYSIWYG block editor for content areas. This hybrid approach meant that the owner could safely update images, text, and products without breaking the design; while ensuring that typography, spacing, colors and module-specific design aspects would have consistency.

Solving the SKU Complexity Problem

The Challenge

With unique, handcrafted items in multiple variants, we risked overwhelming users with hundreds of product listings. How do you organize one-of-a-kind items in a way that feels browseable rather than chaotic?

Research-Driven Information Architecture

I conducted a card sorting exercise with a group of ten potential users, having them group products into categories that made intuitive sense. This revealed two strong patterns:

  • Users grouped by craft type (weaving, metalwork) rather than product type (home goods, jewelry)
  • They wanted to browse by region/culture as well as by craft

Solution: I designed a flexible taxonomy that allowed products to live in multiple categories, giving users different paths to discover items based on their browsing intent.

The Artisan's Choice

In instances where we had a product type that were all unique variants, such as tamo toucan sculptures, it would be impractival to have users sort through a list and images of tens (or even hundreds) of variants. For these cases, I created the "Artisan's Choice" - the SKU and variant would show users samples of the toucan sculpture and explain that users would get a random, unique piece. This turned decision paralysis into a feature, positioning the mystery as part of the adventure and authenticity of the brand.

Optimizing the Purchase Flow

Quick Buy Feature

After launch, I noticed significant drop-off during the multi-step purchase process. Users would add items to cart but abandon before checkout.

My hypothesis:For impulse purchases at markets or when browsing gift items, the friction of a multi-page checkout was killing conversions.

Solution:I designed and implemented (with custom CSS) a "quick buy" option that streamlined single-item purchases into a one-click flow.

Result:This single feature increased conversion rates notably, especially for lower-priced items and gift purchases.

Showcasing the Artisans

The Heart of the Experience

Our biggest differentiator was the direct artisan relationships and the cultural preservation mission. I designed a custom artisan profile module that appeared on every product page, featuring:

  • Artisan photos and names
  • Their craft's cultural significance
  • Links to owner-created documentary videos about their techniques
  • The specific impact of purchases (e.g., "This purchase helps preserve 400-year-old techniques")

This module transformed product pages from transactional spaces into storytelling experiences, directly supporting the brand mission.

Beyond Digital: Designing the Physical Experience

In-Person UX Design

Since Lost Canyon did significant business at museums, galleries, and craft markets, I expanded my UX thinking to physical spaces.

I created a series of user flow sketches for market booths that mapped customer movement patterns through layouts; identified eye-catching sightlines for hero products; and designed product groupings and pathways that made logical sense and encouraged discovery.

Testing & Iteration: Over multiple markets and a standing gallery installation, I observed customer behavior and refined the layouts. This iterative approach led to 30-60% higher sales at recurring events year-over-year.

Packaging & Educational Materials

I designed the complete unboxing and packaging experience, including:

  • Care cards for each craft type that had care instructions, cultural & historical contect, artisan information, and QR codes linking to "how it's made" documentary videos
  • Placards explaining each craft and cultural significance for live events
  • Handouts directing customers to online content, such as the social media channels, site, and videos

These materials ensured every customer—whether online or in-person—left with context and connection to the artisans, increasing repeat purchases and word-of-mouth marketing.

The Impact

Business Growth

  • Online sales now represent ~50% of total revenue, making the digital platform crucial to business sustainability
  • Revenue continues to grow rapidly year-over-year
  • The business has scaled to a sustainable level, and is now looking at methods to expand

Social Mission Success

The real measure of success: artisans have expanded their workshops and hired apprentices thanks to increased demand, particularly for tamo, traditional masks, and silver filigree work.

Lost Canyon is now expanding their artisan roster and reaching into additional South American countries: direct evidence that the e-commerce platform effectively supported the preservation mission.

Design Validation

  • The in-person UX optimizations led to measurably higher sales (30-60% increase at recurring events)
  • The quick-buy feature demonstrably improved conversion rates
  • Customer feedback consistently praised the educational content and storytelling approach
  • The owner has been successfully maintaining and updating the site independently, validating the technical architecture decisions

What I Learned

Mission-driven design is more compelling than purely price-driven strategies.By keeping the artisan stories and social impact at the center of every design decision, we created emotional connections that drove both sales and advocacy.

Constraints breed creativity.Working with a CMS platform and limited budget forced me to be strategic about where to customize and where to accept defaults, ultimately creating a more maintainable solution. It also forced me to be creative and resourceful to find workarounds on truly non-negotiable items./p>

Physical and digital UX are connected.Designing the full customer journey from the online site, to market booth, to product unboxing created a cohesive brand experience that reinforced itself at every touchpoint.

Iteration after launch is where real optimization happens.The quick-buy feature came from observing actual user behavior, not from pre-launch assumptions. Staying engaged post-launch led to measurable improvements.
